## The Question

**Interviewer asks:**

> Why should developers use your platform, and what do you do if they reject it?

## Expected Strong Answer

### Short Answer

> Developers use the platform when it solves their problems better than their current workaround. I would start with developer research, build golden paths for high-friction workflows, measure adoption and satisfaction, preserve escape hatches, and treat rejection as product feedback.

### Strong Answer

> You cannot mandate your way to a successful platform. You need to understand where developers lose time: provisioning, CI/CD setup, security reviews, observability, environments, and production readiness. Then you build golden paths that make those workflows faster and safer than custom solutions. Adoption should be measured through usage, ticket deflection, provisioning success rate, developer satisfaction, and exception requests. If teams reject the platform, I would ask whether the platform is too slow, too restrictive, missing key capabilities, or poorly documented. Some controls are non-negotiable, but the UX and implementation can adapt.

### Deep Dive

Discuss champions, workshops, documentation, migration support, feedback loops, platform roadmap, deprecation policy, exception handling, and ROI.

### Follow-up Questions

- How do you avoid creating another ticket system?
- How do you handle advanced teams who want full control?
- How do you prove platform ROI to leadership?

### Common Mistake

Assuming publishing documentation or creating a portal automatically creates adoption.
